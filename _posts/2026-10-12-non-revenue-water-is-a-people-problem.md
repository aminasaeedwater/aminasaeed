Every utility I've worked with has had the same conversation: the water balance doesn't add up, and the first instinct is to buy something. A new acoustic logger fleet. Smart meters. A software platform with a dashboard.

The kit matters, but the gap between "we installed loggers" and "leakage went down" is almost always about people and process.

## Three things that move the number

1. **Someone owns it.** Loss reduction spread across five departments is loss reduction nobody does.
2. **The data is trusted.** If operators don't believe the district meter readings, they won't act on them.
3. **Repairs are fast.** Finding a leak is only half the job; the clock keeps running until it's fixed.

## What I'd do first

Start with a simple top-down water balance, be honest about the uncertainty in each component, and pick one district to get right before scaling up.
